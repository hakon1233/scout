# Poem or quote of the day, Nordic public domain (needs memory; sample written by hand)

**Width:** one short poem verse or quote a day from authors who died more than 70 years ago
(Norwegian copyright term), with name, work and year, and one plain line of context. Runs daily.
Nothing is quoted from living or recent authors.

**Fits today?** No. A model picks from memory and will repeat favourites and misquote. It needs
(1) a list of what has been sent, (2) a source it can fetch to check the wording, (3) the
"no news" freshness check switched off. A source-fed variant works today: Wikiquote and
Project Runeberg are readable, but they hold no "today" signal.

**Licences:** Ibsen (d. 1906), Bjørnson (d. 1910), Welhaven (d. 1873), Wergeland (d. 1845) are
public domain in Norway. Wikiquote text is CC BY-SA 4.0: link and credit if copied. Do not use
Hamsun (d. 1952; free since 2023), Vesaas, Undset, Jan Erik Vold or any living poet without
checking. Bibel quotes: see `dagens-bibelord-norway.md`.

**Why a starter:** Daily Stoic is a free daily quote email with over 800,000 readers (Reletter
listing, 23 Nov 2026), Lunchtime Poetry and Pome ran as one-poem-a-day mails (Book Riot,
InsideHook). Quote and poem apps charge for personal taste and themes (grief, love, courage).

## Intent doc

```markdown
# Dikt og sitat av dagen

## Reader

A Norwegian reader who wants one short poem verse or quote every morning to read in a minute, themed by the season and day.

## Report

- One quote or 2-4 lines of a poem by a Norwegian or Nordic author who died before 1955
- Author, work, year; one plain sentence on where it comes from
- A match to the day: season, weather, church-year time, a national day (17 May, 6 February, Christmas Eve), or a plain mood
- Check the wording against a fetched page (Wikiquote, Project Runeberg, Wikisource). If it cannot be confirmed, choose another

## Skip

Living authors, anything the model only half remembers, motivational slogans, quotes from the internet with no source, translations by living translators, pieces over 4 lines.

## Sources, check first

- https://no.wikiquote.org/ : quotes by author, with source works; CC BY-SA.
- https://no.wikisource.org/ : full poems of public-domain Norwegian authors.
- https://www.nb.no/ : the National Library; texts out of copyright.

Traps: some Wikiquote pages list quotes with no work; skip those.

## Search terms

<author> dikt, <author> wikisource

## Each story must state

- Author and work, and the year of the work
- The text, 4 lines at most, with the source link under it
- Not on the already-sent list (author + first words)

## No change

Always send one. If no wording can be confirmed, say so and send nothing else.

## Language and place

Norwegian (original spelling, with the date of the edition). Norway, Europe/Oslo.
```

## Sample output (hand-written, 23 Nov 2026; wording to be checked against a source)

**Henrik Ibsen, from the verse he put at the start of his collected poems, 1871**

> At leve er - krig med trolde
> i hjertets og hjernens hvælv.
> At digte - det er at holde
> dommedag over sig selv.

Why today: dark late November, the month when many sit down with their own thoughts. Source to
confirm before sending: Wikiquote "Henrik Ibsen" (CC BY-SA 4.0). Added to sent list: "Ibsen / At
leve er".

## Checks

1. Not sent before (list).
2. Wording matches a fetched source letter for letter.
3. Author died before 1955; edition and spelling stated.
4. At most 4 lines; link given.
5. The day match is stated honestly; no invented anecdote.
6. Norwegian.
