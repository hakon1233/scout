# Norwegian word of the day for learners (needs memory; sample written by hand)

**Width:** one useful word or phrase a day at one level (A2-B1), a plain Norwegian definition,
two short example sentences, and a tip for pronunciation or a false friend. Themed by the
week: November weather, Christmas, work, the kids' kindergarten, dugnad. Runs daily, morning.

**Fits today?** Partly. Web search can pick words from Språkrådet and Bokmålsordboka, but
the run has no list of words already sent and the freshness check treats a dictionary as old.
It needs memory of words sent, a level setting and the "no news" check off. The test for
Norwegian is `norwegian-test-dates.md` (the exam), a different niche.

**Why a starter:** Duolingo had 52.7 million daily users and 12.2 million paid subscribers in
Q4 2025 (SEC 8-K). Many newcomers in Norway need Norwegian for work (Norwegian tests, the
"norskprøve") and no daily product writes for the Norwegian they hear at work and in the
kindergarten. Word-of-the-day mails are a crowded category (InboxReads lists several), so
the edge is the Norway context and a level.

**Licences:** words and short facts are free. Write the definitions and examples yourself. Do not
copy entries from Bokmålsordboka (Språkrådet and UiB) or Store norske leksikon; link to them.

## Intent doc

```markdown
# Norsk ord for dagen, A2-B1

## Reader

An adult learner living in Trondheim who works in English and wants one Norwegian word a day for work, the kindergarten and small talk. Level A2 moving to B1.

## Report

- One word or fixed phrase, with the article and the form (en/ei/et, plural, past tense for verbs)
- A definition in simple Norwegian, then an English gloss
- Two short example sentences from daily life (my own sentences, not copied)
- One tip: pronunciation, a false friend with English, or bokmål vs spoken Trøndersk
- Themed by the week or day (weather, 1 December, kindergarten, work, dugnad)

## Skip

Slang that offends, dialect-only words with no bokmål form, words above B1, long grammar lessons, words already sent, anything copied from a dictionary entry.

## Sources, check first

- https://ordbokene.no/ : Bokmålsordboka and Nynorskordboka; check spelling, article and inflection. Do not copy the definitions.
- https://www.sprakradet.no/ : news and advice on usage, dated.

Traps: ordbokene.no is JavaScript-heavy; if a fetch gives no text, state the word as known and say "not checked".

## Search terms

<ord> bokmål bøyning, <ord> ordbokene

## Each story must state

- The word and its inflection
- Not on the sent list
- The level and theme of the day

## No change

Always send one word. If every candidate was sent, take the next theme.

## Language and place

Write simple Norwegian bokmål with English glosses. Trondheim, Europe/Oslo.
```

## Sample output (hand-written, 23 Nov 2026)

**et peiskos** (A2). Theme: the first frost.

- _Peiskos_ er en god stund foran peisen, gjerne med lys og noe varmt å drikke. English: a
  cosy evening by the fireplace.
- «Det er minus ti ute i kveld. Skal vi ha peiskos?»
- «Vi tente lys og hadde peiskos hele søndagen.»
- Tip: _kos_ is used for almost anything pleasant and cosy; _å kose seg_ = to enjoy yourself.
  Say "kåsj", not "koss".

Sent list: peiskos.

## Checks

1. Not sent before.
2. Inflection and spelling match ordbokene.no (fetch once per word).
3. Examples are original and A2 level.
4. The tip is true; false friends are named.
5. Always one word; no padding.
6. Natural bokmål.
