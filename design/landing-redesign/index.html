01 — Audit · current state

What's weak right now

The live landing page works but reads like a developer's default: monochrome, low contrast in hierarchy, and emotionally flat for a product whose whole pitch is "a calm, personal morning brief." It doesn't earn trust or signal craft on first impression.

Landing page

  • No brand personality. Pure zinc grayscale + Geist. Indistinguishable from a boilerplate Next.js starter — nothing says "Scout."
  • Flat hierarchy. The display heading is only 2.5rem; the eyebrow, h1, and lede sit at similar weights, so the eye has no clear entry point (violates visual hierarchy / F-pattern scanning).
  • Weak emotional & trust signaling. The privacy/local-first story — our strongest differentiator — is buried in a muted caption, not felt (Norman's visceral level; Kano delighters absent).
  • The brief preview undersells the payoff. The artifact users come back for is shown small and styleless; it should be the hero.
  • Generic CTAs, no closing moment. "Open the app" / "View on GitHub" with no reinforcing close, no footer — the page just stops.
  • Density is uniform. Everything is the same spacing rhythm; nothing breathes or accents (no intentional spacing scale moments).

In-app screens (connect / brief / settings)

  • Onboarding/connect is functional but cold — no sense of "you're setting up your own private wire."
  • Brief reading view — the core surface — lacks editorial typographic craft: no real type scale for headlines vs. summaries, weak section rhythm, links undersold.
  • Settings/topics — interest chips read as form controls, not as the expressive "tell Scout what you care about" moment they should be.
02 — Direction · landing page reimagined

Three distinct takes

Same content, same core loop, three different souls. Each is a real rendered mockup — click "Open the live mockup" to interact with the full page.

A · Editorial — "Your private wire service"

★ Recommended

A warm newspaper masthead. Treats the morning brief as what it is — editorial. Serif display (Fraunces), a reading serif for body (Newsreader), mono labels, a compass-needle wordmark, and a single signal-red accent like a dateline. Paper texture, generous rules, a closing "filed every morning" moment.

Open the live mockup →

Why recommended: it's the most ownable and the most on-strategy — it makes the brief the hero and turns "local-first / private" into a feeling (your own wire service, filed for you), not a footnote. Warm, trustworthy, distinctly Scout.

B · Calm — "Local-first, by design"

Alternative

Soft, modern, reassuring. Warm cream canvas, a calm teal accent, Inter + an Instrument Serif italic for warmth. Sticky blurred nav, a centered hero, and a browser device-frame showing the brief in context with privacy badges. The "Linear-clean but human" lane.

Open the live mockup →

Best if the founder wants broad, friendly, mainstream appeal over distinctiveness. Safest, most familiar SaaS-modern feel.

C · Signal — "Runs on your machine"

Alternative

Dark, technical, confident. For the developer/power-user heart of "BYO-model, local-first." Phosphor-green on near-black, Space Grotesk + JetBrains Mono, a live "companion online · localhost:47821" status pill, and the brief shown as a terminal session. Leans hard into the loopback-companion truth.

Open the live mockup →

Strongest with a technical audience; highest personality but narrowest appeal. A dark theme also needs the most accessibility care (contrast on green-on-black was tuned to pass AA).

03 — App pages · what we'd improve

Carrying the direction into the app

The landing sets the tone; these three in-app surfaces are where it has to pay off. Specific elements to improve, aligned to whichever direction is chosen (examples below describe the recommended Direction A; B/C apply the same moves in their own palette).

Onboarding / connect

  • Frame setup as "start your wire," not a config form — warm intro line + the compass mark.
  • Make the companion-connected state a confident, reassuring moment (clear "running on your machine" affirmation).
  • One primary action per step (Hick's law); reduce simultaneous choices.
  • Inline, forgiving validation on any key/field; never a dead-end error.

Brief reading view (highest priority)

  • Real editorial type scale: headline vs. dek vs. summary, not one flat size.
  • Section rhythm with mono kickers + hairline rules (as in the mockup brief card).
  • Stronger source-link treatment and per-item scannability (F-pattern, information scent).
  • A "Good morning, [name] · [date]" masthead so each brief feels filed for you.
  • Calm, content-first density — treat it like a page of a paper, not a dashboard.

Settings / topics

  • Interest chips become expressive, tactile, plain-language — not gray form controls.
  • Clear add/remove affordances with adequate target size (≥44px, WCAG).
  • Group/scan topics so the "tell Scout what you care about" intent is obvious.
  • Visible system status when interests change what the next brief will fetch (Nielsen #1).

Full before/after mockups of these in-app screens are the immediate follow-up once a direction is locked — they inherit the chosen direction's tokens, so building them before the pick would be wasted work.
